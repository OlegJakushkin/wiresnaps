WireSnaps is an independent virtual circuit playground. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by Elenco, the makers of Znatok, Arduino or Microchip. It does not claim official-kit certification or a licence to those companies' trademarks or patents.

Why familiar names appear

Optional reference labels help a learner compare terminal functions and part numbers with equipment they own. The default uses original WireSnaps names. Choosing a reference preset changes labels, never the electrical model.

Snap Circuits and Snapino are names used by Elenco Electronics, LLC. Znatok / Знаток identifies the corresponding construction-kit brand; its ownership and registration territories were not independently established in this review. Arduino is a trademark of Arduino S.r.l. AVR and PIC identify Microchip device families. These names are used only to identify references or emulated devices. No manufacturer logos or certification badges are supplied as app artwork.

The mappings are functional comparisons, not promises of identical mechanical fit, ratings or construction. Znatok numbering currently comes from a secondary copy of a manual and remains provisional. Check your own kit's manual and terminal labels. No current mapping is represented as a verified construction match.

Original explanations and reference locators

The 3D assets are generated from original procedural geometry. Project lessons, instructions and interface artwork are authored for WireSnaps. Screenshots in this guide were captured from the running application.

The inventory keeps part identifiers, original descriptions, source URLs, page numbers and hashes. Copied catalogue excerpts and prices have been removed. Manufacturer manuals, project illustrations, photographs and downloaded research caches are not included in the distributed site or source archive.

This minimisation does not itself settle database rights. A substantial catalogue compilation can raise separate rights questions even where individual facts are unprotected. The full-catalogue data review remains open.

GPLv3+ and third-party licences

The derived application is GPL-3.0-or-later. SimulIDE-derived code retains its original notices; gpsim, simavr and other dependencies retain their applicable licences. GPL does not grant rights to unrelated manufacturers' brands or patents.

The website includes the corresponding source archive and third-party notices. When redistributing a build, keep these materials together and follow the applicable licence terms. Read the full GPL text and third-party notices.

What the IP review establishes

A preliminary patent, trademark, copyright and reference-data review was carried out on 28 September 2026, focused on the current browser software and US, UK and EU concerns. It found relevant patent publications and identified labelling and copied-data issues that have been corrected.

It did not establish a legal finding of infringement or freedom to operate. Patent status, related claims, national validations, confusingly similar marks, design rights and rights in the catalogue require further specialist clearance before relying on an unrestricted commercial launch. The exact WireSnaps search returned no results in the US tool, but the singular WIRESNAP returned an older abandoned application. That is a name-similarity lead, not clearance. The UK registry query could not be completed, and TMview's no-result response does not replace checks of official national registers.
